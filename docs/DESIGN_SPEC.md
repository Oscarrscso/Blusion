# Design specification

UI rules. Preset values live in `AppearancePresets`; haptic defaults live on `HapticPreferences.Place`. Call sites read those models. They do not hard-code a second copy.

## Mac navigation

Draw navigation once at the app root (`MacNavigationScrollState`): the same native segmented picker, width, spacing and start on every page. It follows scroll direction, returns as soon as the user scrolls up, and is always visible at the top. A soft shadow keeps it readable. Pages reserve its space through the shared scroll modifier. Do not add navigation pickers to pages or list rows.

Keep the native tab bar and empty navigation toolbars hidden on Mac, including pushed title, person, catalogue, stream and settings pages. Back-only pages use the shared standalone Back button and do not reserve a bar; its glass matches the navigation control's height. A page at the root of its column has no Back button. Search and Widgets actions sit in scroll content below the shared navigation. Sheets and editors that need native actions use transparent navigation backgrounds.

Discover and Library match category headings. On iPhone and iPad they hide the navigation bar and draw the large title at the page margin. On Mac the type selector at the top of Discover, Library, catalogues and Search uses the navigation control's width and centre. Under it, Discover, Library and catalogues draw their heading in the category font at the selector's leading edge, with their menus ending at its trailing edge, the row exactly as wide as the selector. Mac Settings is a native split view: list beside the chosen page. A page's own sub-pages (Appearance parts, Acknowledgements) open in a column to the right through `SettingsSubpageLink`, with the open row lit and Close at the trailing corner. On iPhone those rows push. Pages whose rows are actions or wide grids (an addon, a widget) still push.

Mac detail artwork uses the same pull-down stretch and scroll-driven zoom as iPhone. Keep scroll observation in the artwork modifiers and the shared navigation overlay. Per-frame offsets must not invalidate the app root or the title's controls and episode list.

Hero artwork on posters uses a 1200-pixel downsample. Cards show grey with the title until that image loads, with no catalogue-poster stand-in. Details start from the cached card image, then the original. A widget's Posters option can inherit Appearance or force the mode; that choice follows the cards into See All and details.

## Mac hover

Every interactive control on Mac has visible hover. Glass controls use `glassHover(_:)` inside the label, after their glass, so fill and rim follow the control's shape. Plain buttons use `pointerFeedback()` or `.buttonStyle(.hoverPlain)`. Cards and rows use `pointerInteraction(cornerRadius:)`. Home's showcase is the exception: no highlight under the pointer; the page chevrons shown on hover are its feedback. A segmented control lights each segment on its own. Native controls keep the system's hover. Settings → Appearance → Hover (`AppearancePreferences.Hover`) drives every value these helpers draw. Defaults are the original look. No call site hard-codes a hover brightness, scale, fill, rim, lift, glow or timing.

## Scroll bars

Use native SwiftUI scroll indicators for vertical views on iPhone and Mac. Flash them on appearance. Keep horizontal indicators hidden on both platforms. Keep native scroll-position bindings for programmatic scrolling. Do not replace the indicator with custom handles or gesture overlays.

## Play-derived control colours

Play and every control derived from its artwork colour use `Theme.playControlColour(hex:)` on iPhone, iPad and Mac. It applies 38% tint opacity once. Do not add a second opacity reduction at a call site.

This covers only surfaces that already used the artwork colour: Play's glass tint, Cast/crew and season picker tints, review sort and AI control tints, stream-switching arrow glass, selected Save/Watched/Trailer icons, and the title Download progress ring. Leave previously neutral icons and circles alone, including inactive action icons, Download's glass circle, shuffle and episode-width controls. Do not add colour to new surfaces when changing opacity. Artwork gradients are not control tints.

When Play's colour treatment changes, update the shared function and every derived control in the same change.

## Appearance

Settings → Appearance applies on iPhone, iPad and Mac. Choices live in `AppearancePreferences`, one JSON string under `settings.appearance`. Defaults live in that model only, and they draw the app as it looked before the page existed. Older saves (stepped card size, corners and spacing, outline as a switch) still decode.

`AppearanceSettingsPage` opens on the preset, then one row per part (Colour, Text, Cards, Corners, Home, Title Pages, Motion & Navigation, and Hover on Mac), then My Looks (Copy and Paste) and Reset. The root has no preview. Each part ends with Restore Defaults for that part (`restoringDefaults(of:)`).

A part previews only what is hard to judge from names, and the preview shows only what that part changes. Previews are the real views (`AppearanceSample` in `AppearancePreview.swift`) with sample titles. Colour shows `TitleActionRow`; Text previews the relevant synopsis, filter bar or heading; Cards the Continue Watching and poster shelves; Corners the Play row, posters, and each opened part's own view; Title Pages a watched and an unstarted `EpisodeRow` while Hide spoilers is on; Hover (Mac) posters, the Play row and the filter bar with one piece lit. Home and Motion & Navigation have none. While a slider is dragged, the value goes to the preview and is saved when the drag ends. VoiceOver and keyboard steps save at once.

- **Colour.** Accent (system colours, the five preset mixes, Blusion, or Custom) is the root tint and the selected landscape tab colour. Background (the named set, Tinted, or Custom) is every page background and the colour `BottomFade` fades artwork into. Tinted is the accent darkened to Tint strength (4% to 30% of its brightness). Accent never replaces Play's artwork colour.
- **Text.** App text size sets Dynamic Type. Body text, Buttons & labels and Titles & headings each have a relative size. Existing per-role Dynamic Type overrides remain editable until linked to body text. Pickers show which attributes follow body text and allow relinking. Width applies to the default system face. Title and shelf fonts use the same heading settings. A preset preserves the listener's Dynamic Type sizes.
- **Corners.** All Corners (0% to 250%) is the shared scale. Each part can follow it or override it; an override survives a change to the shared scale. Season and Cast pickers, rating buttons, Shuffle and the actions beside Play are Other Buttons. Reshape round buttons is opt-in. Circular avatars and progress rings stay circular. Posters and cards scale once through `mediaArtwork(cornerRadius:)`; other shapes use `LayoutMetrics.cornerScale(for:)`, including clipping, outlines and hit areas. Native sort/filter menu borders keep that radius through taps. Settings use native grouped lists. System sheets, menu contents and segmented controls keep native shapes. Segmented controls keep the system's glass track and sliding lens; do not replace their backgrounds or dividers to apply corners.
- **Cards.** Card size (60% to 160%) scales card width, landscape posters, Continue cards and wide-grid columns. Spacing (30% to 200%) scales gaps between cards, shelves, sort menus and filter controls. Columns (2 to 6) is the upright-phone poster grid only. Titles, title lines (one or two, kept even when empty so a row shares a height), capitals, and the genre-year line are caption settings. Tone and Outline go through `mediaArtwork(cornerRadius:)`. Outline buttons (off by default) uses `buttonOutline(_:)` at Default while cards have none. Full-screen hero artwork keeps its own colour. Disc and streamer marks (off by default) end a poster's rating pill and the heroes' metadata line with the best disc from Best Blurays or, when it names none (and for series), the first service in the viewer's region.
- **Home.** Showcase pages turn never, or every 5 to 30 seconds. Small page dots stay in a 20-point row, including for one title; settings and presets cannot hide them. Spotlight height is always `TitleHero.height` (`LayoutMetrics.spotlightHeight`). A title opened from Home must not change size. The spotlight does not turn while Home scrolls, while Home is off screen, or while the pointer rests on it. A page it turns itself plays no haptic. Every page change, including a swipe, restarts the wait.
- **Title pages.** Hide spoilers covers every episode not yet started, with its own switches for stills, synopses and titles; number, date and score stay. Unfold synopsis opens it the first time the page shows. Blurred background (off) runs the hero picture down the page (`TitleBackdropRequest`). Tab bar on title pages is iPhone only, off by default; landscape still hides the tab bar. Section order reorders sections. Eye buttons in those rows show or hide Reviews, Cast & Crew, Information, More Like This and Watch Anywhere; there are no separate visibility switches. Information shows its five most important rows; a tap reveals the rest with the Reviews haptics, including JustWatch rent and buy prices. When Ireland has none, Britain's stand in, converted to euros at the day's ECB rate and marked "≈". On a partly watched title, Play's second line is the time left, ahead of a streamer, disc or episode number. Otherwise the service comes first, then the cheapest rent in the best quality, then a film's best disc. Cast's group control is as wide as a cast card. A movie in a TMDb collection shows the franchise's other films, in release order, above More Like This. Watch Anywhere (off) sits below More Like This; a service logo uses the buttons haptic and opens TMDb's page for the title.
- **Motion and navigation.** Animation speed is a transaction at the root. Off stops every SwiftUI animation, implicit ones included; other speeds scale animations changes carry. System pushes, sheets and the keyboard keep system timing. Zoom transitions off gives every stack no zoom namespace. Open on chooses the tab a plain launch opens; a launch that names a screen still opens there. Tab labels, Shrink tab bar on scroll, and the Continue Watching bar are iPhone only.
- **Presets** set style, not the listener's own choices: colour, text except its size, cards, corners, layout, spotlight timing, unfolded synopsis, section order, zoom transitions, and on iPhone the tab labels and shrinking. They leave text size, custom colours, start tab, Continue Watching bar, which title sections show, spoiler hiding and animation speed. Original is the app's own look. Each preset sets per-part corners, Play's colour (Artwork, White or Accent) and Finish (Glass, Flat or Plain). Flat turns glass buttons into the system's flat bordered buttons and an Artwork Play into tinted grey, never glass. Plain, which no preset sets, is the label alone. The title page's Play is a system rounded rectangle, so its press and hold menu take its corners. Finish reaches every button, including the player's and the tab panel's, through `finishedButtonStyle`, `HoverGlassButtonStyle` and `buttonSurface`. Panels, sliders and indicators keep their glass. The picker shows each preset's background and accent and reads Custom when the look matches none.
- **Layout** is iPhone only; iPad and Mac keep the standard pages. Arthouse sets Editorial (`Views/Editorial`); every other preset sets Standard. Editorial uses a pill bar (`EditorialTabBar`) in place of the tab bar and hides the native bar and Continue Watching bar on every upright page. Title pages hide the pill bar. Landscape keeps the standard title page and the landscape controls.
- **My Looks** stores up to 50 named looks (`AppearanceLooks`, `settings.appearance.looks`). A duplicate name overwrites. Copy puts the look's JSON on the clipboard; Paste reads it back. Reset Settings resets the active look and keeps named looks. Erase Everything removes them.

The root applies app-wide appearance once (`appliesAppearance()` on `RootTabView`). Pages read the background from `\.appAppearance`. Headings use `headingStyle()`; a heading inside a section uses `subheadingStyle()`. Cards, shelves and grids read sizes, gaps, corners and columns from `LayoutMetrics`. Do not read preferences for sizes anywhere else, do not hard-code a page background, and do not give a shelf heading a font of its own. Measured row and grid heights include `AppearancePreferences.layoutKey`. Mac UIKit pages lay their grids out again when it changes. Discover, Library, See All and Search grids fill their width (`fillsWidth`, `GridFill`) so the last column ends on the right margin. On those pages the side margin is `LayoutMetrics.gridPage` for filters and headings too, following Spacing with no floor. Loading placeholders are `CardPlaceholder`s in the card's own shape; on Mac UIKit pages they are `MacGridPage.Part.placeholders`. Search's editorial page keeps its own layout.

iPhone only, each for a reason: Columns, because Mac and iPad grids fit as many columns as the window holds at the card size; Tab labels and Shrink tab bar, because Mac has no tab bar; Continue Watching bar, because Mac has none. Mac only: Hover, because iPhone has no pointer. Everything else on the page is on both platforms.

## Grid pages

Discover, Library, every category page (See All, a genre, a collection, a streaming service) and Search results are one design. A page differs only in its heading, its type selector's choices and its menus' options. A change to one is a change to all of them, on each platform.

They share `LayoutMetrics.gridPage`, the same full-width grid, card gaps and placeholders, and `BrowseFilterBar` as the only filter block. On Mac the type selector sits under the navigation at its width and centre, then one header row as wide as the selector: the heading at its leading edge (a service logo before its name) and the filter menus, each at its own width, ending at its trailing edge. Menus stay on a page without a heading. A heading too long for the row takes the line above the menus. A shelf header's gap separates the row from the selector and the grid. On iPhone the menus are an even grid (two columns, four where they fit) under the page title. A page with nothing to filter draws its heading the same way. Never add a second title style.

## Type, motion and greys

Views take type from `Theme.Typography`: a role (`cardTitle`, `metaLine`, `eyebrow`, `button`) or a step (`large`, `medium`, `small`, `caption`, `micro`, each with `Strong`, and `headline`). Do not write a bare `.font(.footnote)` or similar; chain traits onto the token. Titles and one-off weights get a token when a second view needs them. Fixed-size type on artwork scales through `@ScaledMetric`.

Shared timings are `Theme.Motion` (`standard`, `quick`, `snap`, `fade`, `toggle`). White type over artwork, glass and the player is `Theme.dimText`, `dimmerText` or `faintText`.

## Launch

The launch screen is `LaunchBackground` with `LaunchLogo` (the app icon, rounded, 100 pt) centred. `LaunchSplash` draws the same colour, image, size and centre, and stays over the tabs until Home's first row answers (at most 1.5 s; at once for another start tab), then fades with a slight scale (fade only with Reduce Motion) at the Appearance animation speed. Mac has no launch screen and shows the same splash in its window.

## iPhone landscape controls

Phone landscape uses `LandscapeTabBar`: a compact panel, not the Mac navigation control and not the upright tab bar. A rightward drag opens the whole panel; sliding back keeps it open for tab selection. Home, Search and Back keep `LandscapeTabBarState.edgeInset`. Category pages may put a heading above the controls; root tabs do not repeat one. Title pages and other pushed pages hide the navigation bar and put Back in the top corner.

## Haptics

Every haptic goes through `Haptics.play(_:)` in `DesignSystem/Haptics.swift`, naming a `HapticPreferences.Place`. A call site never names a feel or a number. Defaults live on `HapticPreferences.Place` only. Use the app's places rather than SwiftUI's `.sensoryFeedback`, so the Haptics page reaches every tick. The widget grid's drag is the exception: `.alignment` on Mac and `.impact` on iPhone, because that gesture has no generator to call. Its swap tick is still Pickers & switches.

One haptic per gesture. A control that plays its own must not also take the card's tap. Scrubbing may tick at each ten-second boundary, and brightness or volume swipes at each 10% boundary, once per boundary, never per movement update. Nothing on first appearance, nothing when a value is read from storage, and nothing on a failure the user cannot act on. A page the spotlight turns by itself plays nothing.

Settings → Haptics is iPhone only. Catalyst cannot play UIKit feedback, so the Settings row is hidden on Mac, and shelf code on Mac keeps snapping but skips haptic tracking. The same call sites compile on both platforms; on Mac they do nothing. Saved values are shared. Pointer hover is separate and is not a haptic.

The page has a master switch and Overall strength (25% to 200%), which scales every impact and never lands past full. Places are grouped as Taps, Choices, Player, Motion and Confirmations. Each place has its own switch, feel, impact strength and Restore default. Silence a place with its switch, not with strength 0. Poster shelves, Sliders, Player gestures and Reviews offer single taps only. Player controls, options and gestures have their own section; untuned values use the place defaults, and a saved custom tuning wins. The same player controls cover AetherEngine, mpv and AVPlayer. Try-outs use the real control where one fits, and they are iPhone only.

Adding a place means a case on `HapticPreferences.Place`, a row in the page's groups, and the same call on Mac unless the interaction itself differs.

## Download controls

Keep the title Download button visible while details load and when no usable request or download manager exists. Disable it until it is usable, without moving or resizing it. Stream cards keep their Download button visible and disable it when the manager is unavailable or the stream is ineligible; an existing download keeps pause and resume.
